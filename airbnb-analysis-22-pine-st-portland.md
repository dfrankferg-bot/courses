## 2. The regulatory reality (the deciding factor)

Portland, ME is one of the most restrictive short‑term rental cities in New England. For **2026**:

- **No new non‑owner‑occupied (investor) STRs.** The mainland non‑owner‑occupied cap is set at 1.5% of prior‑year registered long‑term rentals (~293 units), and the city is **not issuing new tenant‑occupied/investor registrations** — only existing operators who renew on time keep their licenses.
- **Owner‑occupied STRs are still allowed** with no citywide cap, but you must prove the unit is your **primary residence** (homestead exemption or tax return).
- **Registration + fire‑safety inspection** required (≈$100 inspection fee), renewed by **Dec 31** each year, and your registration number must appear on **every listing** (Airbnb/VRBO). False info / non‑display can trigger a **$1,000 fine**.
- **Condo association rules are a separate gate.** Many Portland condo associations prohibit or heavily restrict STRs regardless of city rules. **Get the condo docs and confirm STRs are permitted before anything else.**

**Practical implication:** Unless this specific unit comes with a **transferable, grandfathered non‑owner‑occupied registration** (rare, and you'd need to verify transferability with the city), an investor cannot legally run it as a full‑time Airbnb. The viable legal path is **owner‑occupancy**.

## 3. Revenue estimate

Portland is a strong seasonal STR market (peaks in summer/fall; February is weakest), but **small units earn well below the citywide average** — the ~$48K/yr "Portland average" is driven by whole‑home, multi‑bedroom listings.

Realistic figures for a **studio / 1‑BR** in this area:

| Metric | Studio | 1‑Bedroom |
|---|---|---|
| Average daily rate (ADR) | ~$124 | ~$150–200 |
| Occupancy | ~42% | ~34% |
| Implied gross revenue (annual) | **~$19,000** | **~$20,000–24,000** |

Base case used below: **$19,000 gross annual revenue** (studio). A strong operator hitting ~55% occupancy might reach ~$24–25K; that upside is modeled in the sensitivity table.

## 6. The one path that's legal *and* not crazy: owner-occupied

If you would **live here as your primary residence** and rent it short‑term while traveling:

- **Legal:** owner‑occupied STRs remain permitted (register, pass fire inspection, prove residency, confirm condo bylaws allow it).
- **Economics:** renting, say, 90 nights/year at ~$130 ADR ≈ **$10,000–12,000 gross**, offsetting a chunk of your own housing cost. This is supplemental income against a home you're already paying for — a reasonable "house hack," not an investment return.
- You'd also likely qualify for the **homestead exemption**, lowering property tax.

## 7. Recommendation

| If your goal is… | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Buy-and-Airbnb as an **investment** (non-owner-occupied) | **Do not proceed.** Almost certainly not legally operable in Portland in 2026, and the math is negative even if it were. |
| **Live there + Airbnb when away** (owner-occupied) | **Viable** if condo bylaws allow it — modest income offset, not wealth-building. |
| Pure **buy-and-hold long-term rental** | Analyze separately — long-term rents avoid the STR ban but a small West End condo at ~$270K + ~$300 HOA still likely cash-flows negative when financed at 7%. |

**Before spending another dollar of diligence, confirm three things with the listing agent / city:**
1. Does the unit convey a **transferable STR registration**? (Almost certainly no.)
2. Do the **condo association bylaws** permit short-term rentals at all?
3. Exact **unit size, price, HOA fee, and tax bill** — the public data conflicts.
